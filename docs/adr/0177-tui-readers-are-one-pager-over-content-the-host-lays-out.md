# ADR 0177: Readers Are One Pager Over Content The Host Lays Out

## Status

Accepted. Carries out the `Pager` with `Find` control of
[ADR 0151](0151-tui-framework-control-set-and-house-rules.md), applying
its house rules 2, 4, 5, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, and 18, and extends
[ADR 0150](0150-tui-framework-workspace-crate.md) with the framework's
`pager` module; ADR 0150 already answered that the generic pager lives in
the framework and the transcript model stays in Conspectus. Amends
[ADR 0160](0160-tui-keys-one-model-one-character-rule-one-footer-grammar.md):
`PagerKey` gains half pages, find, next and previous match, and help;
`PAGER_KEYS` binds `q` to `Abort` and `Ctrl-D`/`Ctrl-U` as aliases, its
scroll rows are the `PAGER_SCROLL_KEYS` fragment, and `READER_KEYS`,
`PAGER_HELP_KEYS`, and `FIND_KEYS` (`FindKey`) are standard tables.
Amends [ADR 0161](0161-tui-overlays-draw-themselves-on-one-host-owned-stack.md)
decision 7: the pane viewer is the framework's pager, an overlay for any
context, and asks to attach with a request (`Stay(Msg::AttachPane)`) as
before. Amends [ADR 0162](0162-tui-help-pages-one-page-generated-from-key-tables.md)
decisions 6 and 7: the pane viewer's page is generated by the pager, and
both viewers' pages take the footer of a pager's help level
(`PAGER_HELP_KEYS`) instead of the keymap help's, so the parity check
holds each page to its own level's footer. Carries out what
[ADR 0156](0156-tui-scroll-window-follow-rule-and-viewports-by-value.md)
left for this extraction for the pane viewer: its offset is a window with
a sticky end. Amends [ADR 0164](0164-tui-test-kit-key-scripts-frame-driver-goldens-conformance.md):
the conformance suite takes a level's tables consulted after its first
with `conformance::part`.

## Context

The inventory for the framework extraction
(`docs/plans/tui-framework/inventory/05-search-help-viewers.md`, sections
2.2, 3, 5, and 9) found seven scrolling readers of one shape: the
transcript viewer, the pane scrollback viewer, the keymap help, the two
viewers' help pages, the value modal, and the Messages detail, with the
preview pane's region beside them. The two full-screen viewers shared 14
of their 15 to 18 messages, 20 key rows, and 12 help sentences, and still
differed where it shows:

| | Transcript viewer | Pane viewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find unit | a turn | a line |
| Confirming a find | scrolls to the turn, a row above it | scrolls only when the line is off screen, two rows above it |
| Current match | the turn's matches reversed | the line number in the accent |
| `G` | sticks to the end | goes to the last page |
| Notes | `nothing to find yet: press / to search`, silent on no match | `no find: press / to search`, `no match` twice |
| Long lines | wrapped | cut at the edge with no mark |
| Footer | four hand-written fit tiers | two hand-written tiers |

Both drew their help page with the keymap help's footer, `Esc/q/? close`,
while `q` leaves the viewer from there and `Esc` and `?` only go back.
The keymap help and the value modal had no half-page keys.

## Decision

1. **`tuikit::pager` owns the reader.** Its items: `Pager<C>`,
   `PagerContent`, `PagerEffect<M>`, `PagerMode`, `Find`, `NoKeys`, and
   `CONTEXT_ROWS`. `Pager` implements `Overlay<C::Message, Cx>` for any
   context: it reads only its content.

2. **The content is a trait the host implements.** `layout(width,
   theme)` lays the rows out and counts them (called by every draw; a
   content keeps its layout while the width and its state are unchanged),
   `rows(range, theme)` returns the rows of a range, and `header`
   (full-screen header or frame title), `chips` (toggle states, most
   important first), `find` (the units that hold a query), `match_rows` (a
   unit's rows), `paint` (the matches on the rows drawn), `find_detail`
   (what the chip adds when nothing shown matches), `describe` (the
   content's words for a pager key on the help page), `empty`, `apply` (a
   content key, answered with `None`, `Relayout`, `Close`, or
   `Request(M)`), and `copy` (the copy hook) complete it, most with
   defaults. Its keys are a const table, `KEYS`, resolved after the
   pager's; its help sections go before the pager's last one and its
   footer segments before the keys that leave. `scroll::WrappedRows` gains
   `hanging`, `cut`, and `line_of` for contents that number, wrap, or cut
   their lines.

3. **Three modes.** `Screen` fills the screen with a header, a rule, the
   body, a rule, and a one-row footer led by the `[ n/m ]` position chip.
   `Modal(size)` draws in a `ModalFrame` of a named size, titled with the
   header, with the shared scrollbar, the find chip or prompt, and the
   footer kept on the frame's last rows. `Embedded` is the body and the
   scrollbar in an area the host lays out, whose keys the host routes
   through the pager's scroll calls.

4. **One window, clamped and kept, with a sticky end.** The pager keeps
   the offset asked for and the window the last draw showed: page keys
   move by its rows and half pages by half of them (at least one); every
   draw brings the offset inside the content and keeps it, so a key past
   an end is undone by the next. `G` (and `at_end`) makes the window keep
   to the end on every draw, as the content grows, the screen is resized,
   or a toggle lays the rows out again; a key that moves the window, or a
   find landing, lets go.

5. **One find (house rule 18).** `/` opens a prompt on the one line editor
   holding the active query; `Enter` asks the content for the units that
   hold the query and goes to the first one whose rows reach the window's
   top, and the next draw always scrolls it to one row below the top
   (`CONTEXT_ROWS`), shown or not; `n` and `N` step and scroll the same
   way, wrapping at the ends with an info note. An empty query clears the
   find; `n` with no find says `nothing to find yet: press / to search`
   (a warning); `n` over no match says nothing, since the chip says `no
   match`. The chip reads `find "q" 2/5`, the query cut to 17 cells.
   `Find::paint` draws every match in the theme's match style and the
   current one with the theme's `selection_active` modifier over the text,
   the transcript viewer's look, now from the theme. `land(query, unit)`
   opens at the match nearest a unit found elsewhere.

6. **Levels and leaving (house rule 4).** `Esc` steps back: the help page,
   the prompt (keeping the active find), the active find, then the pager
   (`Close`). `q` and `Ctrl-C` leave from any level (`Dismiss`), and `q`
   types in the prompt. A note is a `Feedback` the footer shows in its
   severity's tone until the next key; the host sets one with `set_note`.

7. **Keys.** `PagerKey` gains `HalfPageDown`, `HalfPageUp`, `Find`,
   `FindNext`, `FindPrev`, and `Help`. `PAGER_SCROLL_KEYS` holds the
   scroll rows (a fragment with no `Ctrl-C`), which `PAGER_KEYS` and
   `PAGER_HELP_KEYS` include. `PAGER_KEYS` binds `Esc` to `Close` and `q`
   to `Abort`, both shown as `close`, so its footer is unchanged;
   `READER_KEYS` adds `/` find, `n`/`N`, and `?` help and is the pager's
   default; `PAGER_HELP_KEYS` is the help level's (`j/k/PgDn/PgUp scroll ·
   Esc/? back · q close`); `FIND_KEYS` is the prompt's (`Enter find · Esc
   cancel`). A host gives a framed reader opened by a key its own table
   over `PagerKey` (that key as another close, `y` as `Copy`), and the
   pager has a find or a help page only when its table binds one.

8. **Generated help and footer (house rule 8).** `?` opens a `HelpPage`
   from the pager's table and the content's, in the content's words where
   it has its own, with the help level's footer. The footer is the pager
   table's scroll keys, its other keys, the content's keys, then the keys
   that leave; in full-screen mode it follows the position chip, the find
   chip, the note, and the content's chips, and when it does not fit it
   drops the scroll keys, then the other keys, then the chips from the
   last, then the content's keys, keeping the keys that leave and cutting
   what remains with `…`. The header is cut with `…` too.

9. **The pane viewer is a content.** `Pager<PaneScrollback>`
   (`PaneViewer`): the lines numbered in a five-cell gutter and wrapped
   under it (`w` cuts them at the edge instead, with the `wrap·on (w)`
   chip saying which), a find unit of one line, the current line's number
   in the accent beside the shared current-match look, the header (the
   mux, window, and pane, the command, the line count), the pager's ends
   and find described as the oldest and newest line and a find in the
   scrollback, and `a` asking to attach. `PaneViewerState`,
   `PaneViewerMsg`, `PaneViewerOutcome`, `PANE_VIEWER_KEYS`, its help
   generator use, its scroll and find code, its hand-written footer, and
   its `Overlay` impl are removed; its own table is `PANE_KEYS`.

10. **The viewers' help footers.** Both viewers' pages take the footer of
    `PAGER_HELP_KEYS`, generated from the keys their help level takes.

## Consequences

- **Visible changes in the pane viewer** (the CSP-745 summary lists them):
  long lines wrap under their number and `w` cuts them with `…`; every find
  landing (confirm, `n`, `N`, and the match it opens on) puts the line one
  row below the top; the current match is drawn reversed; the footer is
  generated and fitted (`wrap·on (w) · j/k/PgDn/PgUp scroll · / find · ?
  help · a attach · Esc/q close`); the position chip counts screen rows;
  the header is cut with `…`; the notes take the pager's words and
  severities (a wrap note is green); the help page lists `w`.
- **In both viewers** the help page's footer reads `j/k/PgDn/PgUp scroll
  · Esc/? back · q close`. **In the keymap help and the value modal**
  `Ctrl-D`/`Ctrl-U` move half a page; their footers are unchanged.
- The transcript viewer, the value modal, the help page's body, the
  Messages detail, and the preview region move onto the pager in their own
  stories without a trait change: the control's page sketches each port.
- A reader added later is a content and a table; it cannot drift from the
  others' paging, find, leaving, help, or footer.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Scroll long lines sideways** (`h`/`l`, a column offset). It keeps the
  capture's layout, but a find match past the edge needs the view to move
  sideways to it, and the transcript viewer already wraps. Wrapping is the
  default, the `w` toggle keeps the cut view for wide tables and box
  drawing, and a column offset is a seam.
- **Keep the pane viewer's landing** (scroll only when the match is off
  screen, two rows of context). Steadier when matches are near each
  other, but house rule 18 says a confirmed find scrolls to its match, and
  one rule for both viewers is what a user can rely on.
- **Mark the current match in a gutter** (the pointer mark on its first
  row). It works without color, but moves every row two cells for a
  reader with no cursor; the selection look marks it in place, as the
  transcript viewer did.
- **Count lines, not rows, in the position chip.** Closer to the header's
  `N lines`, but the window scrolls by rows, and the transcript viewer
  counts rows; one rule for the chip wins.
- **A separate action enum for the pager** beside `PagerKey`. It would
  leave the help page and the value modal on one pager enum and the
  readers on another; one enum and one base table let those move onto the
  pager with a table each.
- **Resolve the content's keys first.** It would let a content take a
  pager key, which the footer and the conventions cannot see; the content
  takes only keys the pager leaves free.
- **Keep the viewers as nested reducers** (a message per key through the
  host's reducer). The pane viewer handled its keys itself already; an
  overlay for any context needs no host message for scrolling.

## Open Questions Answered

- *Wrap or scroll sideways?* Wrap, with `w` to cut at the edge.
- *Which find unit?* The content's: a line in the pane viewer, a turn in
  the transcript viewer.
- *Does confirming always scroll?* Yes, with one row of context above.
- *Which current-match marker?* The theme's `selection_active` over the
  match, with the pane viewer's accent line number kept beside it.
- *What does `G` do?* Sticks to the end in every pager.
- *Which footer does a viewer's help page show?* Its level's, from
  `PAGER_HELP_KEYS`.
